It is the final weeks of the Second World War in the Pacific. You are about to step into the position of one of three people who, across a few weeks in 1945, made decisions that ended it.

At each point, you'll be given the real situation as it stood, informed by what this person valued, believed, and had lived through — then asked to decide. Afterwards you'll find out what actually happened, and how close your call came to history. Play as all three to build a full summary you can print.

Some choices lead to the path history actually took. Others lead to a hypothetical — a historian-informed guess at what might have followed, clearly marked as speculation. This explores perspective and decision-making; it is not a definitive record of "what would have happened."

What Actually Happened

On 6 August, the atomic bomb "Little Boy" destroyed Hiroshima; an estimated 70,000–135,000 people died. On 9 August, "Fat Man" destroyed Nagasaki, killing an estimated 39,000–80,000 — the vast majority civilians. These remain among the deadliest single events in human history.

On 15 August 1945, Emperor Hirohito's recorded address — the Gyokuon-hōsō — was broadcast to the Japanese people, the first time most had heard his voice. He described "enduring the unendurable and suffering what is insufferable." On 2 September, Japan's formal surrender was signed aboard the USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay, ending the Second World War.
